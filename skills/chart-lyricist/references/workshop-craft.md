# Workshop Craft Synthesis

Use this reference when drafting or revising lyrics. These are working methods synthesized from current songwriting programs and practitioner workshops, not a claim that one formula guarantees a hit.

## Generation before judgment

- Berklee's Pat Pattison curriculum starts with object writing, then develops diction, rhythm, rhyme, structure, sectional contrast, forward motion, and revision. Generate sense-bound material before selecting lyric lines.
- Andrea Stolpe's destination writing uses sight, sound, touch, taste, smell, and movement tied to a place, person, and time. Keep the first freewrite unedited; apply restraint later.
- Separate generative and editorial passes. Premature polishing usually produces safe abstractions.

## Prosody

- Align lyric, melody, harmony, rhythm, phrasing, vocal delivery, and production with the central emotion.
- Speak every line naturally. Mark lexical stresses; do not place weak syllables on the musical accent unless the dislocation is intentional.
- Treat rhyme like punctuation. Perfect rhyme can close; family rhyme, assonance, consonance, or withheld rhyme can sustain tension.
- Choose rhyme words for meaning first. Reject the first predictable pair and build a wider phonetic field.

## Dramatic architecture

- Give the singer a want, a present-tense situation, and a cost. Character is revealed by action, omission, diction, and choices.
- Define the job of every section. Escalate or reframe; do not restate.
- Let verses supply evidence, a pre-chorus change pressure or trajectory, the chorus crystallize the governing idea, and the bridge alter the dramatic equation.
- Build contrast through title placement, line length, rhyme sound, melodic range, note duration, rhythm, vocal entry, density, or emotional power.

## Hooks and revision

- The hook is the conceptual common denominator, not necessarily a slogan. It must fit the grammar and scene naturally while gaining meaning from repetition.
- Replace broad declarations with observable evidence, but preserve direct telling where a chorus needs a clean emotional claim.
- Delete padding. Each line must advance scene, character, hook, sound, or turn.
- Test: could another singer, in another town and relationship, sing this unchanged? If yes, add identity-bearing detail.
- Simulate four workshop readers: a first-time listener, the singer, a ruthless editor, and the producer. Revise disagreements rather than averaging them away.
