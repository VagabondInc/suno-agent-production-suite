# Experimental Genre-Fusion Corpus

These 72 original recipes are compositional seeds, not fixed prompts or invented claims about established genres. Select one host grammar, borrow only the named guest traits, and expand it into the exact 1000-character production brief. Keep cultural terms specific and respectful; research unfamiliar traditions before publishing.

## Electroacoustic and Club Hybrids

1. **Concrete Footwork** — Chicago footwork host at 160 BPM; tape-spliced machinery, contact-mic clicks, and granular room tone replace conventional percussion; clipped alto chants.
2. **Free-Jazz Post-Dubstep** — half-time 138 BPM post-dubstep pulse; bass clarinet multiphonics and trumpet smears improvise above negative-space sub bass.
3. **Acid Chamber Breaks** — fractured 132 BPM breaks with TB-303 lead; string quartet supplies stabbing counterpoint and tense cluster swells.
4. **Prepared-Piano Microhouse** — 122 BPM microhouse host; prepared-piano ticks become hi-hats, felted bass notes answer a dry spoken vocal.
5. **Electroacoustic Neo-Trance** — 136 BPM trance architecture; bowed metal, breath noise, and hydrophone textures replace supersaw layers until the final lift.
6. **Musique-Concrète Ambient Dub** — 72 BPM dub space; found doors, kettles, and platform announcements feed tape delays around a single sub pulse.
7. **Granular Gqom Nocturne** — sparse gqom host with lopsided low drums; granular piano dust and nocturne harmony create suspended tension.
8. **Deconstructed Club Madrigal** — discontinuous club impacts and silence; four-part madrigal counterpoint is chopped, formant-shifted, and spatially scattered.
9. **Spectral Techno** — 128 BPM minimal techno host; harmonic spectra from bells determine chords, filters, and evolving metallic overtones.
10. **Wonky Glitch-Funk in 7/4** — rubbery syncopated bass, 7/4 drum machine, micro-edited claps, and crooked clavinet; playful falsetto interruptions.
11. **Electroacoustic Jersey Club** — triplet kick patterns frame bowed cymbal, scraped strings, and breath percussion; hook appears as chopped syllables.
12. **Dark Ambient Baltimore Breaks** — Baltimore club callouts and breaks emerge from low drones, industrial ventilation, and distant choral clusters.

## Adaptation Method

1. Choose one recipe whose host genre matches the user's desired listening context.
2. Keep the host's pulse and form; replace guest traits to suit the topic and vocal identity.
3. Decide the exact section where each guest element appears. Global stacking causes mud.
4. Add a lyrical concept that makes the sonic contrast meaningful rather than merely strange.
5. Translate the result into the production-brief order in `style-syntax.md`.
6. Use two or three local tags from `tag-corpus.md` to mark the contrast points.
7. Validate character limits, then describe the result as an experiment—not a guaranteed Suno behavior.
